Spend and audience are not the same thing

RCN Capital runs 10 live ads to an Instagram audience of 166. It is buying reach outright rather than building it — which fits a strategy aimed at brokers, a few hundred of whom are worth more than a large consumer following. LendingOne is the mirror image: a comparable ad count on top of 8,100 followers.

And six that spend nothing at all

Every lender below was checked against Meta’s Ad Library by numeric page ID, across all ad statuses and all time. Each returned zero ads, ever:

CoreVest · Easy Street Capital · Roc360 · Upright (formerly Fund That Flip) · Velocity Mortgage Capital · Longhorn Investments

Anchor Loans belongs on that list too — it posts organically but has never bought a paid ad. Among the named national lenders, silence is normal. But that is not the whole market, and the next section is the correction.

The long tail

The majors are quiet. The category is not.

Searching Meta’s Ad Library by keyword rather than by company changes the picture completely. A single sweep of four product keywords plus eighteen named lenders returns 781 live ads from 249 advertisers. Almost none come from the national platforms. They come from regional shops, brokers and individual loan officers — and their copy is sharper than anything the big lenders are running.

Why this is the most useful table here

On Meta, Trilith is not competing with the giants. It is competing with Capital Fund 1, Griffin Funding and a few hundred regional shops — while CoreVest has funded $7.8 billion and never run a single ad, and Anchor has never bought one either.

A balance sheet does not win attention here. That is a fight a founder-led lender with real deal math can actually win, and it is the strongest argument on this page for moving now.

What this changes

The competitive picture is not an empty field. It is dozens of small operators fighting over four pain points — tax returns, DTI, speed, and the hard-money-to-DSCR refinance — while the large national lenders sit it out.

Two of those angles are the sharpest writing anywhere in this research, and neither is on the board above. The refi trap — you won the deal with hard money and are now bleeding on the rate — is a live pain that maps exactly onto Trilith’s bridge-to-DSCR product, and only one small shop is naming it. Arithmetic as the hook — “take your rate, subtract 5.375%, multiply by your balance” — is the creator-format finding from earlier in this document, already being run as a paid ad by a competitor.

The format that separates them

Creators post arithmetic. Lenders post claims. Sean Pan’s top video is a line-item P&L: $4,750 rent, minus $475 management, $410 landscaping, $1,021 mortgage, $953 escrow — $1,890 a month. That is the whole video.

His BRRRR post is more pointed still. He narrates taking a private money loan for the rehab, refinancing at 75% of a $330,000 appraisal, recovering every dollar he put in plus $30,000 tax-free, and keeping a cash-flowing property. That is Trilith’s product, told end to end, by someone who does not sell it.

And every top performer closes the same way — a comment keyword. CALCULATOR, MARKETS, MASTERCLASS, WAITLIST, PODCAST, “free leads”, KIAVI. Trilith’s four funded case studies contain exactly this kind of arithmetic — $370,900, 7.875%, 70% LTV — published as static pages with no narration and no way to capture the reader.

The conclusion

What the evidence adds up to

Five conclusions, ordered by how much each one should change what Trilith does. Every number below is on this page.

01

Nobody owns attention in this category

Balance-sheet share and share of voice have come apart completely. CoreVest has funded $7.8 billion across 35,000 projects and has never run a single ad. Anchor Loans is a top-ten originator and has never bought one either. Kiavi originates about $8B a year — the largest private lender in the country — and runs 33 live ads. A regional shop in Scottsdale runs 114.

There is no incumbent in attention to displace, because the firms with the capital to dominate it have declined to show up. Trilith is not trying to out-shout a giant; it is walking into a room the giants left.

02

Almost everyone rents attention. Almost nobody builds it

RCN Capital runs roughly fifteen live ads to an Instagram audience of 166 people. That is not incompetence, it is a strategy — buying reach monthly rather than accumulating it. The category default is an audience that resets to zero the day the budget stops.

The counter-example: Anchor’s brand page has 4,700 followers. Their CEO’s personal Instagram has 41,600. Nine to one, same company — the person outperforms the institution.

03

The format that wins is not the format lenders use

Creators out-reach lenders by ten to a hundred times, and the winning content is not clever. Sean Pan’s top video is a profit-and-loss statement read aloud: rent, minus management, minus landscaping, minus mortgage, minus escrow, equals $1,890. Lenders publish claims. Creators publish arithmetic.

Trilith’s four funded deals carry exactly that arithmetic — $370,900, 7.875%, 70% LTV — published as static spec sheets. Creators have to borrow or reconstruct deals like these. Trilith funded them. It owns the format that wins, with authority nobody in the comparison has, in the one shape guaranteed not to travel.

04

Four pains are saturated. Two are open, and they are Trilith’s

Tax returns, DTI, speed, income documentation — every lender says the same four things, often in the same words. Two pains nobody is funding: the refi trap (“you took the hard money loan to win the deal; now the rate is still running and the property has stabilized”), which maps onto bridge-to-DSCR, and mid-project abandonment — the lender who gets cold feet at the draw, not at the close.

Anchor told the mid-project story once, organically. It reached 89 people.

05

Every call to action is an ask for a conversation

Nobody runs “Apply.” The literal button text is Learn More, Get Offer, Get Estimate, See My Rate. The category has concluded the ad’s job is to earn a phone call — which means “the team that answers the phone” is not a differentiator, it is the category’s business model. Everyone is competing for the same call.

The gap is that Trilith asks for the call without earning it. Every competitor has a capture mechanic; Kiavi’s best asset in ninety days was a comment-to-DM offer. Trilith has 23 blog posts, four case studies, and no way to turn a reader into a contact.

The position

Trilith is not competing with Kiavi. It is competing with Capital Fund 1 and a few hundred regional shops — and it can win, because it has two things they do not: founders worth putting on camera, and real funded deals with real numbers.

The sequence the evidence supports is narrow. Publish the deal math already owned. Put a named human behind it. Aim it at the two unclaimed pains rather than the four saturated ones. Give people a way to raise their hand.

None of that requires outspending anyone. It requires publishing what already exists, in the format the data says works.

What this evidence cannot tell you

This is Meta only. A lender running Google Search, LinkedIn, direct mail, conference sponsorship or pure broker relationships shows as silent here. The six lenders that have never advertised may be spending heavily where this cannot see — CoreVest’s $7.8 billion did not come from nowhere.

Ad count is not spend, and days-in-market is not performance. Capital Fund 1 running 114 ads to Kiavi’s 33 does not mean it outspends Kiavi; Meta publishes neither budget nor results. A long-running ad is probably working — it is not proof. And 166 followers may be exactly the 166 brokers RCN wants.

The two conclusions that hold regardless of what happens off-platform are the first and the third: the attention vacuum and the format gap. Both are about what is being published, not about what is missing.
