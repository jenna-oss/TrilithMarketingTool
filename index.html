The long tail

The majors are quiet. The category is not.

Searching Meta’s Ad Library by keyword rather than by company changes the picture completely. “fix and flip loan” returns roughly 1,600 live ads; “DSCR loan” roughly 1,100. Almost none come from the lenders on the board above. They come from regional shops, brokers and individual loan officers — and their copy is sharper than anything the platforms are running.

What this changes

The competitive picture is not an empty field. It is dozens of small operators fighting over four pain points — tax returns, DTI, speed, and the hard-money-to-DSCR refinance — while the large national lenders sit it out.

Two of those angles are the sharpest writing anywhere in this research, and neither is on the board above. The refi trap — you won the deal with hard money and are now bleeding on the rate — is a live pain that maps exactly onto Trilith’s bridge-to-DSCR product, and only one small shop is naming it. Arithmetic as the hook — “take your rate, subtract 5.375%, multiply by your balance” — is the creator-format finding from earlier in this document, already being run as a paid ad by a competitor.

The format that separates them

Creators post arithmetic. Lenders post claims. Sean Pan’s top video is a line-item P&L: $4,750 rent, minus $475 management, $410 landscaping, $1,021 mortgage, $953 escrow — $1,890 a month. That is the whole video.

His BRRRR post is more pointed still. He narrates taking a private money loan for the rehab, refinancing at 75% of a $330,000 appraisal, recovering every dollar he put in plus $30,000 tax-free, and keeping a cash-flowing property. That is Trilith’s product, told end to end, by someone who does not sell it.

And every top performer closes the same way — a comment keyword. CALCULATOR, MARKETS, MASTERCLASS, WAITLIST, PODCAST, “free leads”, KIAVI. Trilith’s four funded case studies contain exactly this kind of arithmetic — $370,900, 7.875%, 70% LTV — published as static pages with no narration and no way to capture the reader.

Where the room is

Eight positions, ranked by how much room is left

Order carries the argument. The first two are genuinely uncontested. The broker position has moved — it was the cheapest win on this list until RCN Capital turned up buying it.

01

Spanish-language investor lending

No US investor lender in the corpus runs a single Spanish creative — not Kiavi, Lima One, Visio, New Silver or Anchor. Trilith has already built and paid for a complete Spanish mirror of all 60 pages and is getting nothing back from it.

Demand signal — one Spanish-language real estate educator, 243K followers: 63K / 19.8K / 14.9K views. Kiavi’s best organic post in 90 days: 5.7K. Caveat: that audience’s US share is unverified and should be tested before committing.

02

Publish the arithmetic they already own

The best-performing format in this entire audience is line-item deal math, and not one lender uses it. Trilith has four funded deals with real numbers sitting as static pages. A creator gets 16,300 views narrating a BRRRR financed with private money; Trilith actually funds those deals and publishes them as a spec sheet.

Sean Pan’s 4plex P&L: 59.8K views. Trilith’s Baltimore portfolio — $370,900, 7.875%, 70% LTV, four properties — currently has no narration, no breakdown and no capture.

03

Macro-to-micro market commentary

Trilith’s blog already covers the SpaceX IPO’s liquidity effects, the Iran conflict, the housing act, the Figure/Kiavi deal. Not one lender in the corpus posts macro commentary on social — the whole category posts product features. This is Trilith’s most distinctive existing asset, locked in 4–10 minute reads.

Nearest attempts — Anchor’s single housing-orders post (119 views), and Temple View, which runs one market-outlook ad asking how serious investors can capitalise. Thin, but no longer zero: treat this as a two-ad head start to beat, not empty ground.

04

The founder as the channel

Anchor Loans’ brand Facebook has 4.7K followers. Their CEO’s personal Instagram has 41.6K — nine times the brand, same company. New Silver runs zero paid and is entirely their founder to camera. Kiavi, owned by a $717M acquirer, structurally cannot answer this.

Trilith’s asset — an existing Levine Capital webinar library, unclipped.

05

“Your lender just got acquired”

Figure bought Kiavi for $717M. Kiavi is running a competitor-takeover landing page right now. Trilith already published the sharpest take on that acquisition — a borrower with a tight close and a phased rehab needs more than a portal. Nobody is running the reverse argument.

Most time-sensitive item here. The window closes as the story ages.

06

A real broker-facing brand — now contested

Visio points two of its four landing pages at brokers and Anchor runs broker testimonials, but RCN Capital changes the picture: every one of its ~15 active ads targets brokers, and it has been buying that position since June. It works a pain nobody else touches — not the borrower’s deal, the broker’s commission and pipeline.

Trilith still has a live broker portal with no brand, no landing page and no content behind it. The infrastructure is built; the position is no longer free. Also note the collision — RCN’s landing page reads “Skip the Bank’s Red Tape” against Trilith’s “No red tape.” That phrase now has budget behind it and should be replaced.

07

First-time flippers

All four of Kiavi’s testimonial hooks are beginner fear. The category leader spends its heaviest budget on people doing their first deal. Trilith’s homepage says “not first-time homebuyers”; the FAQ says first-time flippers can get funded. That contradiction has to be resolved before anything else scales.

A positioning decision, not a content play. It blocks 01 — the audiences overlap.

08

Any capture mechanic at all

Kiavi’s best-performing asset of the last 90 days was a comment-to-DM keyword offer. Trilith’s CTAs are “Talk to a Loan Officer” and “Get a Quote” — no keyword, no lead magnet, no offer, no deadline. Trilith could win every argument above and still capture nobody.

Not positioning — but it is why positioning will not pay without it.

How this was built, and what it does not prove

Two sources. The Spyglass advertising corpus (Meta, Instagram, TikTok) for the five indexed lenders and the creator set, cross-read against each lender’s live site and landing pages. Meta’s public Ad Library for advertisers Spyglass does not index.

The lender category is only five deep in Spyglass. Thirty named private lenders were searched by name and a substring sweep run across the whole corpus; the US investor lenders in it are Kiavi, Lima One, Visio, New Silver and Anchor. That is a limit of the corpus, not of the market — RCN Capital is on this page precisely because it is advertising heavily and is entirely absent from it.

Days-in-market is a proxy, not a metric. Platforms do not publish spend or performance. A long-running ad is probably working; it is not proof.

The Ad Library sweep is resolved. Ten lenders were checked by numeric Facebook page ID rather than by name — name search fuzzy-matches badly, returning sauna vests for “CoreVest” and cash-advance apps for “LendingOne”. Page IDs were read from each company’s own Facebook page, reached via the social link on its website. Four advertise: RCN Capital, LendingOne, Renovo Financial and Temple View Capital. Six have never run an ad. Dominion Financial could not be resolved — its linked Facebook page does not load — so it remains unchecked rather than clear.

Absence is still not proof of absence. This covers Meta only. A lender running Google, LinkedIn, direct mail, conference or broker-channel marketing would show as silent here.

One figure needs validating before it is acted on. The Spanish-language demand signal comes from creator accounts whose audience geography is not verified in this data. Test cheaply before committing.
